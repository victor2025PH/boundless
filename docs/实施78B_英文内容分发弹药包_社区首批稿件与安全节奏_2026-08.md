# 实施78B · 英文内容分发弹药包：社区首批稿件与安全节奏（2026-08-28）

> 实施78 的 **P1-5** 执行件。解决的是全站最便宜的一个问题：**内容早就有了，只是从没送出去过**。
>
> 事实基线：官网已有全套 `/en` + 5 个英文对比页（现在还各自有了专属分享图），
> 但 `/en` 30 天只有 **24 次浏览**，可归因流量 **100% 来自我们自己的 TG 频道**。
> 这一份不需要再生产内容，只需要把已有的送到有人看的地方。

---

## 零、先记三条纪律，违反任何一条都不如不发

1. **Reddit 不是发链接的地方**。新号直接贴产品链接 = 秒删 + 可能连累域名被子版规则拉黑。
   正确姿势：**先答有用的问题，产品只作为「其中一个选项」出现，并主动披露身份**。
2. **披露身份是加分不是减分**。「利益相关：我是 ChatX 的开发者之一」这句话，在 Reddit /
   知乎 / Indie Hackers 都会显著提高可信度；被别人扒出来才是灾难。
3. **不写业绩数字**（和实施77B / 78A 同一条红线）。我们能说的是能力与价格结构，
   不能说「帮客户增长了 X%」——没有可审计的证据。

**节奏上限**（比内容质量更重要）：同一个 Reddit 账号**每周最多 1 次**提到自己的产品，
其余时间只做纯帮助性回答；账号 karma < 50 之前**一次都不要提**。宁可慢三个月，不要被封。

## 一、UTM 约定（不带 UTM 的分发等于没发）

站点分析按 `source/medium/campaign` 三段拼串记录（现有数据形如
`telegram/channel/daily-0806`）。社区分发统一用：

```
?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=<thread-or-post-slug>
?utm_source=quora&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=<question-slug>
?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=<article-slug>
?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<post-slug>
?utm_source=indiehackers&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=<post-slug>
```

**落地页优先级**：能落 `/en/compare/<竞品>` 就别落首页——对比页是我们内容最强、
转化件最全的一页（导航壳 / 面包屑 / 首屏 CTA / 相关对比互链齐备）。

## 二、平台打法与首批稿件

### 2.1 Reddit（最慢、但引用权重最高）

**目标子版**（按相关度，先潜水读一周再开口）：
`r/ecommerce`、`r/dropship`、`r/FulfillmentByAmazon`、`r/smallbusiness`、
`r/Entrepreneur`、`r/SaaS`、`r/SideProject`（后两个对「我做了个东西」相对友好）。

**稿件 R1 — 回答型（用于「有什么工具能统一管多个聊天平台」类帖子）**

```
We hit this exact wall selling into SEA — the messages were split across Telegram,
WhatsApp, Messenger, LINE and Zalo, and half of them weren't in English.

Two things I'd check before picking a tool:

1) Which channels are *actually* covered. Most Western tools are WhatsApp-first and
   quietly skip LINE (Thailand) and Zalo (Vietnam). If your customers are there, that
   gap is the whole problem, not a nice-to-have.
2) How you're billed. Per-seat or per-monthly-active-contact pricing punishes you exactly
   when things go well. Usage-based is easier to reason about at small scale.

Options people usually land on: respond.io and SleekFlow if you want a mature cloud
help desk; Wati if you're WhatsApp-only; Chatwoot if you want open source and don't mind
running it yourself.

Disclosure: I work on ChatX, which is another option in this space — desktop app, covers
those six channels including Zalo/LINE, and replies in the customer's language. Happy to
answer questions about any of the above, including where ours is the wrong fit (it's
Windows-only right now, and if you need official WhatsApp Business API compliance from
day one, the cloud SaaS tools are a better match).
```

**稿件 R2 — 回答型（用于「客服工具太贵 / Intercom 涨价」类帖子）**

```
Worth separating two costs that usually get lumped together: the seat cost and the
AI/message cost.

Seat-based pricing made sense when the tool was a shared inbox for a team. If most of your
replies are drafted by AI, you're paying for seats you don't really use — and per-MAC
(monthly active contact) pricing is worse, because it charges you more precisely when
your marketing works.

Things to ask a vendor: is there a floor I pay even at zero volume? What happens to my
chat history and customer profiles if I leave? Is translation metered separately?

Disclosure: I build one of the tools in this space (ChatX). We went usage-based partly
because of the above — but the honest caveat is that usage-based gets *less* predictable
at high volume, so if you have steady heavy traffic, a flat plan may genuinely cost less.
```

**稿件 R3 — 发帖型（仅限 r/SideProject / r/SaaS，且账号已有 karma）**

标题：`We made a desktop AI inbox that covers Zalo and LINE, not just WhatsApp — here's what we learned`

```
Building for Southeast Asia taught us that "omnichannel" usually means "WhatsApp plus
whatever's easy." Thailand runs on LINE, Vietnam runs on Zalo, and most tooling treats
both as afterthoughts.

Three things that surprised us:

1. The hard part isn't connecting channels, it's identity. The same buyer shows up as a
   phone number on WhatsApp, a @handle on Telegram and a display name on LINE. Merging
   those without wrongly merging two different people took longer than every integration
   combined.
2. Translation quality is a conversion feature, not a convenience. Machine-translated
   replies read as spam to buyers; that shows up in reply rates, not in any BLEU score.
3. Desktop was the right call for our users, and the wrong call for our marketing —
   most of our traffic is mobile, and a Windows-only download is a dead end on a phone.
   We ended up adding a "take this link to your computer" step.

Happy to go into any of it. Disclosure: this is our product (ChatX), link in profile
rather than here since that's the sub's convention — ask and I'll drop it.
```

### 2.2 Quora（写一次长期有流量，AI 引用率也高）

**目标问题**（搜这些，认领已有高浏览问题优先于新建）：
- "What is the best customer service software for a small e-commerce business?"
- "What are good alternatives to Intercom for small businesses?"
- "How can I manage WhatsApp, Telegram and Messenger messages in one place?"
- "What AI tools can reply to customers in their own language?"

**稿件 Q1**（结构直接复用 `/en/compare` 的五维框架，这是我们最强的中立内容）

```
Before comparing products, answer three questions — they eliminate most of the market
for you:

1. Where does the data live? Chat history and customer profiles are the asset. Cloud SaaS
   is fine for many teams, but check the export path before you commit, not after.
2. What do you actually need the AI to do? Answering tickets and nurturing a relationship
   over weeks are different products. Most "AI customer service" tools do the first.
3. How are you billed? Per seat, per monthly active contact, or per usage. At small scale
   usage-based is usually cheapest and easiest to predict; at steady high volume, flat
   plans win.

Concretely: respond.io and SleekFlow are mature cloud options; Wati is WhatsApp-first;
Chatwoot is the open-source route if you can host it. If your buyers are in Thailand or
Vietnam, check LINE and Zalo coverage specifically — that's where most tools stop.

(Disclosure: I work on ChatX, which is a self-hostable option in this category. I've tried
to describe the trade-offs rather than sell; the comparison I maintain is at
bd2026.cc/en/compare and it lists where each competitor is the better pick.)
```

### 2.3 Medium / dev.to（长文，喂 Google 与 AI 引用池）

**首篇选题**：`Why "omnichannel" usually means "WhatsApp and whatever's easy"`
（把 R3 的三点扩成 1,200-1,500 字，加上真实截图 —— 素材见 `website/public/products/screenshots/`）

**结构**（照抄即可）：
1. 开篇 60 字直接给结论（AI 引擎抽的就是这一段）
2. 「东南亚的渠道现实」：泰国 LINE、越南 Zalo、印尼菲律宾 WhatsApp/Messenger
3. 「身份合并才是难点」——技术细节，建立可信度
4. 「翻译质量是转化功能」
5. 「我们做错的一件事」：桌面端 + 移动端流量的错配（**真实自曝，这段最值钱**）
6. 结尾一句话披露身份 + 链接到 `/en/compare`

### 2.4 LinkedIn（B2B 可信度，转化低但对目录站/合作方有用）

```
Most "omnichannel" customer tools are WhatsApp-first. That works until your buyers are in
Thailand (LINE) or Vietnam (Zalo).

We spent the last year building for exactly those markets, and the thing that surprised us
wasn't the integrations — it was identity. The same buyer is a phone number on WhatsApp, a
@handle on Telegram and a display name on LINE, and merging those *without* wrongly merging
two different people is harder than every channel integration combined.

If you're evaluating tools for cross-border sales, the three questions worth asking are:
where does my data live, what do I need the AI to actually do, and what am I billed per.

I maintain a comparison of five tools in this space (including where ours is the wrong
choice): bd2026.cc/en/compare
```
